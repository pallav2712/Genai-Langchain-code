**The set‑up:** “Why did the cricket team bring a ladder to the match?”

**The punchline:** “Because they heard the batsmen were always *going over the top!*”

**Why it’s funny – the wordplay**

| Phrase in the punchline | What it usually means | How it fits the cricket world |
|--------------------------|----------------------|------------------------------|
| **“Going over the top”** | 1. *Literally* – climbing above something, which would need a ladder. <br>2. *Figuratively* – doing something in an exaggerated, overly dramatic way. | In cricket a **batsman** tries to hit the ball *over* the **top** of the boundary rope for a six. So “going over the top” can be read as “sending the ball over the top of the field.” |
| **“Batsmen”** | The players who use a bat. | The joke pretends the batsmen are *always* hitting the ball so high that it goes over the top of the stadium, so the fielding side would need a ladder to retrieve it. |

So the humor comes from **two simultaneous meanings**:

1. **Literal** – If the batsmen really were sending the ball over the roof, a ladder would be a sensible (though absurd) tool to get it back down.  
2. **Figurative** – “Going over the top” is an idiom for being excessive; the joke pretends the batsmen are *excessively* good at hitting high shots.

The absurd image of a cricket team hauling a ladder onto the pitch, combined with the double‑meaning of “going over the top,” creates the punchline’s surprise and makes it funny. The cricket emoji 🏏 reinforces the sport‑specific context, and the smiling face 😄 signals that it’s all in good fun.
